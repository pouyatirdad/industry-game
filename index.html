    The book

    Clearing fund

    How the exchange works Anybody may post an ask (I will sell) or a bid (I will buy). The exchange pairs a bid with an ask whenever the buyer's price covers the seller's plus the freight and the fee, and the pair becomes a supply contract like any other. You can also take any listing outright at the price posted. The house charges both sides a small clearing fee on every settlement, and that fund is what a government borrows from when it cannot make payroll.

    Supply contracts

    How a contract works A contract moves a fixed quantity at a price fixed on the day you sign, every n ticks for its term, and it is settled before your own people are served — so promising away supply you do not have really will starve them. If either side falls short, whoever fell short pays the other half the value it failed to deliver or take. Nothing else moves goods between nations: no cargo leaves without a contract behind it.

      How the score is worked out Every nation is measured against the best in the world on six counts and the score is the weighted total out of 100: the size of its economy (30), the industry it has built (20), what that industry turns out per tick (20), how well its own people are supplied (15), its treasury (10) and its trade balance (5). Click a column to rank by it.

      Licences offered to you

      The tree

      How research works Your laboratories are funded as a share of the tax base — the slider above — and turn treasury into research points at a fixed rate. Pick a subject and it completes when its points are banked; the overflow carries into the next one. Anything you have not learned, you can licence from a nation that has, if you hold a pact with it: that costs more than researching it but arrives at once, and everything upstream you are missing comes with it. The forty-five other governments do all of this too, and being first up the tree is what lets you sell licences to them.
